Research programme

Overcoming the programming wall.

A heterogeneous system uses more than one kind of processor: a general-purpose CPU to run an operating system, and accelerators such as GPUs and FPGAs to do specific work faster and more efficiently. In the post-Moore era these machines carry the largest applications we have — deep learning and quantum software platforms among them. Extracting performance proportional to the resources in such a system is the problem we work on.

RESEARCH GOALS

Accelerate large-scale applications, pragmatically

The programming wall is an obstacle for general programmers and deep learning model designers who want to parallelize and optimize their applications across the processor resources they already have. We attack it with deep learning models together with compiler, runtime, architecture and operating system techniques at every level.

Areas

Execution platforms for deep learning

As the computation and memory requirements of deep neural networks grow, multi-GPU and multi-node training and inference have become essential — and parallelizing that work requires sophisticated knowledge of the underlying architecture. We build the systems that remove that requirement.

01

Heterogeneous accelerators and system optimization

Making a mixed pile of accelerators behave like one machine

TCCL · ASPLOS 2024DeepUM · ASPLOS 2023SOFF · ISCA 2020
  • Efficient communication across mixed NVIDIA and AMD GPU environments (HetCCL)
  • Using CPU and GPU memory together through CUDA Unified Memory
  • Optimizing AI computation on accelerators of every kind, NPUs and FPGAs among them
  • Tensor Core-based FFT and convolution optimization
  • GEMM optimization on NPUs
  • FPGA–GPU hybrid execution
02

Distributed inference and cluster operation

Serving large models on clusters that are shared, and metered

SPipe · PACT 2025
  • Making MoE inference more efficient by easing the imbalance between computation and communication
  • Tracking energy consumption across a GPU cluster
  • Resource scheduling tuned to the usage patterns actually observed

Large language models

Work on the models themselves: how fast they generate, how much context they hold, how their tokenizers are built, and whether a benchmark score means what it claims to.

01

Next-generation language and multimodal models

Generating more than one token at a time

  • Parallel token generation, to raise the inference efficiency of large language modelsJacobi decoding and speculative decoding for parallel generation and faster inference.
  • Efficient text generation with diffusion language models (DLM)Converting autoregressive models to diffusion models, and optimizing parallel decoding through masking and remasking.
  • Multimodal language models that fuse data of several kindsJoint training over medical imaging, ECG and electronic medical records, for better predictive performance.
02

Long-context LLMs and multimodal training

Holding a long document without losing the thread

TRONTOR · EMNLP 2026
  • Improving long-context models through positional-encoding control and attention-sink analysis
  • Extending long-context modelling techniques to the multimodal setting
  • Multimodal models specialised for the medical domain
03

Areas

Quantum computing

An end-to-end software stack for a machine that does not exist at scale yet, and a simulator that stands in for it — built on the observation that storage is cheap and DRAM is not.

01

High-performance quantum–classical hybrid computing

Simulating, exactly, what the hardware cannot yet run

SnuQS · ICS 2022
  • A platform that simulates large quantum circuits exactly, on classical computing resources
  • Storage offloadingOnly the regions a computation needs are loaded onto the GPU, which gets past the DRAM capacity limit.
  • Compiler and runtime in concertThe circuit structure is analysed so that qubit placement and the data-movement schedule are decided at compile time.

Practice

How the work gets done

Measured, not asserted

Every systems claim comes with an end-to-end number on hardware we describe. Microbenchmarks are evidence, not conclusions.

Artifacts by default

Code, configurations and traces are released with the paper. If a result cannot be reproduced from the repository, it is not finished.

Two departments, one review

Systems drafts get read by model people and the reverse. It is slower, and it catches the assumptions each side forgot it was making.

Looking for a problem to work on?

Each thread above has open questions we would happily hand to a new student or a visiting collaborator.
